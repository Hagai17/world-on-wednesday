# Candidates & honest field assessment — news-image-20261125

## The entry

"The World on Wednesday": a classic broadsheet front page for Wednesday
25 November 2026 carrying **55 sourced stories** (counted on the page:
3 leads + 3 medium stories + 15 numbered digest items + 34 regional/wire
one-liners), a UTC timeline strip, a numbered region atlas, per-story
CONFIRMED/REPORTED labels with named sources, a DATE CHECK box excluding
older news, and a markets ticker. 1024×1024, 349,310 bytes.

## Drafts considered

The density playbook (winners study §6) was followed deliberately; the
alternatives weighed were:

1. **Illustrated scenes** (the field's fashionable lane: "12–13 drawn
   scenes", "bespoke editorial gouache") — rejected: drawn scenes carry
   fewer verifiable facts per square inch, risk garbled/implausible
   details, and our past illustrated entries scored worse than dense
   broadsheets.
2. **Fewer, bigger stories** (8–10 leads only) — rejected: criterion 2
   rewards selection/breadth; the 20261118 winner won on volume with
   attribution (51 stories).
3. **Dense broadsheet (chosen)** — maximises sourced story count and
   per-story attribution, keeps every claim checkable, and reads as a
   real newspaper at full size.

Five render iterations were needed to fit the full page (footer included)
without cutting content; a stat strip replaced an unreadable diagram on
the mine story.

## Field sample (submissions API, page 1 of the live field)

Top competing titles advertise: "35 stories first reported Wednesday,
10 regions, 13 drawn scenes, how-sure labels", "36 verified full-day
stories, 9 regions, 12 beats, 9 bespoke editorial gouache scenes",
"Day 329 World Briefing (25 Nov 2026)", and several broadsheet-style
pages leading with the same verified leads used here (West Java mine,
Cairo tanker fire, UK energy cap).

## Honest comparison — no rounding up

- **Density:** ours advertises 55 stories vs the field's advertised
  35–36 — ahead on raw count, *if* the judge credits one-liners equally
  with full stories, which is not guaranteed; several of ours are
  single-line wire items.
- **Sourcing/labels:** even. The leading rivals also use how-sure labels
  and named regions; ours names a source on every numbered story and
  adds a DATE CHECK box, which none of the sampled titles mention.
- **Design/craft:** behind the best rivals. The gouache/scene entries
  are more distinctive visually; ours is a disciplined but plain
  text broadsheet, and the Hormuz schematic is small. At ~300px the
  masthead, date and lead headlines read clearly; body text does not
  (true of every dense entry, including the 20261118 winner).
- **Fidelity:** strong — every number on the page was cross-checked
  against the day's reporting during research (see research notes);
  claims are hedged exactly where the wires hedged (Houthi claim is
  REPORTED, not CONFIRMED).

Net: a credible top-decile-density entry whose realistic edge is story
count, attribution and date discipline; its realistic weakness is
visual distinctiveness against the illustrated leaders. Estimated
duel rate vs the sampled top-5 titles: ~0.50–0.60 — competitive, not
dominant. Stated plainly rather than rounded up, per the winners-study
lesson that provisional confidence misleads.
